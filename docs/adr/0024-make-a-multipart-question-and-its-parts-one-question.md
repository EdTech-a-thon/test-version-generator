# Make a Multipart question and its Parts one Question

A teacher sent a Regents-style block: a passage about the Ottoman Empire's decline with its source line, followed by two Multiple Choice questions a student answers from it. The source tests they are converting are full of these — a passage, a quote, a diagram or a table, then questions that are meaningless without it. Today there is nowhere to put one; `/extract` sends it to Unconverted Questions.

`'multipart'` is therefore a fifth Question Type, Multipart, last in Section order. One Multipart question holds a stem — free rich text, usually the shared material, with any source line typed as ordinary content rather than a field of its own — and an ordered list of Parts, each either Multiple Choice or Short Answer. Modelling the shared material as a record that ordinary Questions point to was rejected for the reason ADR 0021 gave for Matching: the stem is what makes the Parts one thing, and an Exam holding a Part without its stem would print a question nobody can answer. The Question Bank keeps a Multipart question whole, and an Exam adds, Removes and Replaces it as one Question. True/False and Matching Parts, and a Multipart question inside a Part, were left out: nobody has asked for them, and a whole Matching set inside a Part has no sensible layout. A True/False Part could be added later without disturbing anything here.

The type was first named Stimulus, after the material at its top. It was renamed Multipart before it shipped: what sets it apart is that it has Parts, and its stem need not be a passage at all.

On the test, a Multipart question takes one number, and its Parts print lettered beneath it — `12.` then `a.`, `b.` — rather than one number per Part as Matching does. Neither the question nor a Part prints an answer blank: each Part is set in a short step under the stem by its letter alone. It prints in its own Multipart section because its Parts mix types and a Section is one type; the section's directions are generic. Parts never shuffle, since they are lettered in place and often build on one another; Vary moves a whole Multipart question among the others in its section, and shuffles each Multiple Choice Part's answers by stable choice id as it does a Multiple Choice question's. Answer columns and Work Space are set per Part on the exam sheet, exactly as they are per question, and a Short Answer Part without Work Space gets a Short Answer question's default.

Difficulty and Topics belong to the Multipart question, because the bank finds Questions and a Part is not one. The Answer Key gives a Multipart question one entry under its number with a line per Part: a letter for a Multiple Choice Part, the Suggested Answer for a Short Answer Part. A Multipart question with no Parts, like a Multiple Choice question with no correct answer, is incomplete rather than invalid.

The editor lays a Multipart question out as the paper does, with as little nesting as it can. The stem is written unnested at the top, where any question's stem is. Below it a "Parts" heading, ruled full width, stands over every Part, with "Add Part" at its right end and again after the last Part; either offers Multiple Choice or Short Answer. Each Part is one dashed box that reads as a question of its type: a lightly shaded header set as the question editor's front matter is — the Type label, then the Part's type badge — with controls to move it up or down and × to delete it at the right; then, ruled off in the same dashes, its stem; then the existing Multiple Choice answers or Suggested Answer component as the box's last cells. The editor does not letter Parts, since their order is what letters them on the paper, and Parts are not dragged: an editor-built drag handle beside the editor's own proved more confusing than two arrows. The type badge opens a menu that switches a Part between Multiple Choice and Short Answer at any time; its stem stays, and the answers of the type it leaves are set aside for the rest of the editing session, so switching back brings them again — but only the answers of the type it is are ever saved. A new Multipart question starts with one blank Multiple Choice Part. Deleting the last Part leaves only the heading and "Add Part"; the Parts node and each Part's answers component regrow if deleted, as a True/False pair and a Matching set do. The bank lists a Multipart question by its first line and its count of Parts, and search matches text in any Part.

The stem and Part a are kept together on a page where they fit. After that the plan breaks only between Parts, never inside one, and a stem taller than a page may split between its blocks as a last resort. No "(continued)" cue is printed.

The exchange format follows the rule ADRs 0020 and 0021 followed: a `0.3.0` consumer must reject an unknown Question Type, so the Question Bank Record becomes `0.4.0`, published beside the earlier versions. A record `multipart` Question carries its `stem`, as every record Question does, and `parts`, each `{id, type, stem}` plus `choices` for a Multiple Choice Part or an optional `suggestedAnswer` for a Short Answer Part; Part ids are `qN-sM` and their choices `qN-sM-cK`, clear of the ids Multiple Choice and Matching already use. Part columns stay out of the record, as a question's do. The Exam package does not yet carry per-Part presentation — a Part's answer order, columns and Work Space — so an Exam imported from one gives every Part its defaults. `/extract` converts a "Base your answers to questions 12 and 13 on…" block into one Multipart question even though the source numbers each Part, and it leaves a block containing a Part of any other type in Unconverted Questions rather than forcing it.
